# Cost of inaction opens deals, it does not close them

Author: Jen Allen-Knuth
Source: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/demandjen1_cost-of-inaction-coi-isnt-best-used-to-activity-7409260573252841472-Jr0q
Published: December 23, 2025 (about five and a half months old when checked)
Date checked: 8 and 9 June 2026 (IST)

---

Cost of Inaction (COI) isn't best used to close a deal. But, it's great for opening one.

There are two major decisions a buyer needs to make in a buying process:

#1 - Of all the fires we need to fight in our business, is this problem worth solving, and worth solving now?

#2 - Are you the right partner to solve it with? And is this the right solution to solve it?

If the prospect is eager to solve the problem your solution solves, you probably don't need to use COI with your prospect. They've already decided it's a problem worth solving and solving now. You MIGHT need it when it comes to helping your champion convey the need to act with the rest of the buying group. But, the bigger problem here is usually a lack of decision confidence in the best way to solve it.

COI is best used when prospects believe how they're solving the problem is "good enough". It's geared at addressing job #1.

Here's an example to distinguish the two.

Most of us use shampoo (hopefully).

If I think my current shampoo is good enough, a "better" shampoo pitch won't make a difference. I perceive what I'm using today as good enough to get the job done. Here, I'd need to illuminate a problem with status quo (my current shampoo) before pitching a solution. That's COI.

But, if I KNOW I need a new shampoo, I go to the store. I walk down the aisle and see 100 different shampoos. 70% of them sound like they could work for me. Now, I'm stuck. There are so many different options. The prospect is stuck on job #2. That's an indecision problem.

The JOLT Effect book exposed the difference between the two. It's a phenomenal read for anyone losing deals to no decision.

The reason I teach COI is because much of my Sales training is focused on how to open deals in an outbound motion.

I grew up in Sales selling intangible solutions that weren't high-demand.

No one has ever said "man, are we almost out of functional best practice research?".

To earn a swing at those opportunities, we had to expose a negative consequence of execs making opinion-based decisions. It wasn't an obvious problem.

Both jobs-to-be-done matter. Like most things in Sales, it's about knowing the right time and place to use the right approach! 👊
