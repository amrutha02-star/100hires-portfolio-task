# Audit your own sequence before it sends

Author: Petr Kaliuzhny
Source: https://www.linkedin.com/posts/kaliuzhnypeter_heres-what-people-who-sell-outbound-for-activity-7462411999054921728-5Dha
Published: May 19, 2026 (about three weeks old when checked)
Date checked: 8 and 9 June 2026 (IST)

---

Here's what people who sell outbound for a living are actually sending in 2026.
Fair warning: you might recognize your own sequence in this.

1️⃣ Formatting.

Three blank lines between every sentence. That's not style - it's an automation default nobody checked.

If you didn't read your own sequence, why would I?

2️⃣ Fake personalization.

"Most B2B companies in Europe grow through referrals and warm intros."

Generic - and wrong for who you're emailing. You're pitching outbound to an outbound software company, on the premise we don't do outbound.

If you must go generic, at least pick something sharp and often true - like "outbound software companies are usually the cobbler with no shoes." That one would've landed.

3️⃣ Weak proof.

"We helped a 20-person managed services provider book 21 demos."

No name. Not a company I can picture, not even in my world. Proof I can't map to myself isn't proof.

4️⃣ Jargon.

MSP. RFP.

I read it three times and still had to work out what MSP meant - and only because I'm writing this post. Your reader won't. Make them decode you and they're gone.

5️⃣ Wrong audience.

"Enterprise buyers take 4-6 months to pick an MSP."

Not us, not our customers. I lose interest on the first obvious line and never reach your actual point - which was fine: get in early, before the RFP. Buried under a pitch for someone else.

6️⃣ The irony.

You sell "fully managed campaigns to reach your ideal buyers."

I'm about as ideal-fit as it gets - an outbound software company. Yet the whole pitch is built for enterprise, and we're SMB. You didn't even get the segment right on a perfect-fit lead. The outreach is the demo, and the demo just broke trust.

7️⃣ The breakup.

"Last one from me, I promise. Reach out whenever."

Nothing changed between email 2 and 3. No new angle, no reason - that's not a follow-up, it's filler. And spare me the "I promise" - that line doesn't open a door, it tells me you already closed it.

None of it is hard:

- Read your own sequence before it sends.
- Generic is fine - but pick a premise that isn't true of everyone.
- One proof they see themselves in.
- Plain words, no abbreviations to decode.
- Right ICP, or don't send.
- Every follow-up earns the reply with something new.

Look - nobody who actually does the work avoids mistakes. I screw up plenty myself.

The problem isn't the mistake. It's that most of "GTM" either don't know how, or can't be bothered to do it properly.
